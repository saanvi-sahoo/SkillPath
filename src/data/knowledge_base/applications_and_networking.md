# Applications, Cover Letters and Networking Guide

## Writing a cover letter
Keep it to one page and three short parts: why this company and role (be specific), two achievements that match the job description's top requirements with numbers, and a closing line asking for a conversation. Reuse the job description's exact skill names where they are true for you. For internships, projects and coursework can replace work experience.

## LinkedIn profile that recruiters find
Use a headline with your target role and top skills (for example "Aspiring Data Analyst | SQL, Power BI, Python"), a professional photo, an About section in 4-5 lines, and the Skills section filled with the keywords recruiters search. Add projects with links, request two or three recommendations, and post occasionally about what you are learning.

## Referrals, cold emails and internships
Referrals have much higher response rates than cold applications. Ask alumni or connections for a referral with a short message that names the exact job ID and why you fit. Cold emails to founders or hiring managers should be under 120 words: who you are, one relevant achievement, a specific ask, and a link to your portfolio. Apply for internships 2-4 months before the season and track every application in a spreadsheet.

## Handling rejections
Rejection is normal in job search; conversion rates of 5-10% from application to interview are common for freshers. Ask for feedback when possible, review which applications got responses, and adjust your resume keywords and targeting rather than applying more of the same.
