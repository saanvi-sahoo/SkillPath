# Resume and ATS Guide

## How Applicant Tracking Systems work
An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) parses a resume into fields (contact, education, experience, skills) and ranks candidates by how closely their text matches the job description. Keyword overlap with the job description's required skills, job titles and tools is the strongest signal. Unusual layouts (tables, text boxes, multi-column designs, images, headers/footers) often break parsing, so key information can be lost before a human ever reads it.

## Raising an ATS match score
Mirror the exact wording of the job description where it is truthful (for example "Power BI" not just "BI tools"). Put a dedicated Skills section near the top. Use standard section titles: Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education, Certifications. Save as a text-based PDF or DOCX, never a scanned image. Spell out acronyms once, e.g. "Natural Language Processing (NLP)". Do not keyword-stuff or add skills you cannot discuss in an interview; recruiters verify.

## Writing strong bullet points
Use the pattern Action verb + what you did + measurable result: "Built a Power BI sales dashboard used by 12 managers, cutting weekly reporting time by 40%." Prefer numbers (%, time saved, users, revenue, accuracy). Keep 3-5 bullets per role or project and put the most relevant first. Freshers should lead with projects, internships, hackathons and coursework that match the target role.

## Resume length and structure
Freshers and candidates with under 5 years of experience should keep one page. Order: contact line with LinkedIn/GitHub, 2-3 line summary targeted at the role, skills, experience or projects, education, certifications. Tailor the summary and the top skills for every application.
