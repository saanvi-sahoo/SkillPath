# Career Planning Guide

## Choosing a career direction
Match three things: what you enjoy doing day to day, what you are or can become good at, and what the job market pays for. Read 10-20 real job descriptions for a role to see the skills that repeat, and talk to people already in the role (LinkedIn informational interviews).

## Entry-level tech and data career paths
Common entry points: Data Analyst (SQL, Excel, Power BI/Tableau, statistics, communication), Business Analyst (requirements, process mapping, SQL, stakeholder communication), Software Developer (one language deeply, DSA, Git, a framework, databases), Data Scientist (Python, statistics, machine learning, experimentation), Cloud/DevOps Engineer (Linux, networking, a cloud provider, CI/CD, containers), QA/Test Engineer (testing fundamentals, automation with Selenium or Playwright). Analyst roles often lead to data science or analytics engineering; developer roles lead to senior engineering, architecture or engineering management.

## Switching careers
Identify transferable skills from your current role (domain knowledge, communication, project management). Close the gap with a focused 3-6 month learning plan and 2-3 portfolio projects in the new field, ideally using data or problems from your current domain. Consider bridge roles that combine old and new expertise.

## Job search strategy
Apply to roles where you meet roughly 60-70% of the listed requirements. Combine job boards (LinkedIn, Naukri, company career pages) with referrals, which have much higher response rates. Keep your LinkedIn headline and skills aligned with your target role, and post about your projects. Quality and tailoring beat mass applying.

## Salary and offers
Research ranges on Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, Levels.fyi or LinkedIn Salary for your role, city and experience. Consider total compensation (fixed, variable, benefits, learning opportunities). It is normal and professional to negotiate politely once you have a written offer.
