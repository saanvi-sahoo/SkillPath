# Student Well-being and Productivity Guide

## Managing exam stress
Stress is easier to manage with a plan: break the syllabus into daily targets, keep a fixed sleep schedule (7-8 hours), and practise under exam-like conditions so the real exam feels familiar. Short breaks, physical activity and slow breathing (for example 4 seconds in, 6 seconds out) reduce anxiety. If stress becomes overwhelming or affects daily life, talk to a trusted person, a college counsellor or a mental-health helpline.

## Beating procrastination and staying motivated
Start with a 10-minute task to overcome the starting barrier, use time blocks (Pomodoro: 25 minutes focus, 5 minutes break), and keep your phone out of reach while studying. Track visible progress (a checklist or streak) and study with a peer for accountability. Motivation follows action: small daily wins matter more than occasional long sessions.

## Time management for students and working learners
Plan the week on Sunday: fixed commitments first, then 1-2 focused study blocks per day at your most alert time. Working learners can use 60-90 minute weekday blocks and longer weekend sessions; protect them like meetings. Review the plan weekly and cut low-value activities rather than sleep.

## Studying effectively for long-term exams and placements
Mix learning and testing: after each topic, solve questions without notes, keep an error log, and revisit weak topics with spaced repetition. For placements, combine aptitude practice, core-subject revision and coding or domain practice every week instead of finishing one area at a time.
